# RSA Public Key tests


## Motivation

The security of RSA relies on the hardness of factoring the public modulus
$n = p \cdot q$ into its secret prime factors $p$ and $q$. When RSA keys
are generated by faulty software or hardware implementations—such as
insufficiently seeded pseudorandom number generators (PRNGs), flawed prime
construction algorithms, or memory corruption bugs—the resulting moduli often
exhibit algebraic or statistical structure that allows efficient factorization
or detection using only the public key $(n, e)$.

Paranoid divides RSA public key checks into two categories:

*   **Single Key Checks (`rsa_single_checks.py`):** Tests that inspect each RSA
    public key independently for weak parameters, known vulnerable PRNGs,
    special-case factorizations, or structural biases in $p$ and $q$.
*   **Aggregate Checks (`rsa_aggregate_checks.py`):** Tests that analyze large
    batches of RSA public keys together to detect shared prime factors or shared
    subgroup structure across distinct keys.

## Single Key Checks

### CheckSizes

Checks whether the bit length of the RSA modulus $n$ is smaller than the
recommended minimum of 2048 bits (`gmpy.bit_length(n) < 2048`). Moduli shorter
than 2048 bits provide less than 112 bits of security and are flagged with
`SEVERITY_MEDIUM`.

### CheckExponents

Checks whether the public exponent $e$ differs from the standard Fermat prime
$F_4 = 65537 = 2^{16} + 1$. Small exponents (such as $e = 3$) can be
vulnerable to padding or broadcast attacks when combined with weak padding
schemes, while abnormally large or non-standard exponents may indicate broken
key generation or attempts to hide small private exponents $d$. Keys with
$e \neq 65537$ are flagged with `SEVERITY_MEDIUM`.

### CheckROCA

Checks whether the modulus $n$ is affected by the Return of Coppersmith's
Attack ([ROCA], CVE-2017-15361), a vulnerability in the Infineon RSALib prime
generation implementation. Vulnerable primes have the structure

$$p = k \cdot M + \left(65537^a \bmod M\right),$$

where $M = \prod_{i=1}^{k} p_i$ is the primorial (product of the first $k$
consecutive primes). Because both $p$ and $q$ are congruent to powers of
$65537$ modulo every prime factor $r \mid M$, their product
$n = p \cdot q$ must also lie in the multiplicative subgroup generated by
$65537$ modulo each small prime $r \mid M$.

`ROCAKeyDetector` tests whether $n \bmod r$ is a power of $65537 \bmod r$
for the small primes dividing $M$:

*   For moduli with `n.bit_length() <= 512`, Infineon's implementation used the
    primorial up to $r = 167$ (excluding $173$), so the detector checks
    primes up to $167$ to avoid false negatives on 512-bit ROCA keys.
*   For larger moduli (`n.bit_length() > 512`), the detector checks all primes
    up to $173$.

Keys failing this check are flagged with `SEVERITY_HIGH`.

### CheckROCAVariant

Checks for keys generated by ROCA-like constructions that use an unknown base
$g$ instead of $65537$:

$$p \equiv g^{r_1} \pmod{M}, \quad q \equiv g^{r_2} \pmod{M}.$$

Whenever $r_1 \equiv r_2 \pmod{2}$ (which occurs with probability $1/2$ for
random exponents $r_1, r_2$), the product $n \equiv g^{r_1 + r_2} \pmod{M}$
is an even power of $g$ and therefore a quadratic residue modulo every prime
factor of $M$, regardless of the choice of base $g$.

`ROCAKeyVariantDetector` tests whether $n$ is a quadratic residue modulo each
of the 48 smallest primes greater than 3 (from 5 up to 229). A randomly
generated RSA modulus passes all 48 quadratic residuosity checks with
probability approximately $2^{-48}$. Keys already detected by `CheckROCA` are
excluded so that only novel variants are flagged (`SEVERITY_MEDIUM`).

### CheckFermat

Attempts to factor $n = p \cdot q$ using Fermat's factorization method (e.g.,
CVE-2022-26320), which writes an odd modulus $n$ as the difference of two
squares with $a = (p + q) / 2$ and $b = |p - q| / 2$:

$$n = a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b).$$

When $p$ and $q$ are close, $a = \lceil \sqrt{n} \rceil + k$ for a small
non-negative integer $k$, so $a^2 - n = b^2$ is a perfect square. Starting
from $a_0 = \lceil \sqrt{n} \rceil$, the implementation tests up to
`max_steps` (default `100,000`) steps using a modulo-16 / modulo-8 residue
sieve: for any odd $n = p q$, the residue $n \bmod 16$ restricts
$a \bmod 8$ to at most two valid residue classes, allowing the search loop to
step directly between valid candidates. Factored keys are flagged with
`SEVERITY_CRITICAL`.

### CheckHighAndLowBitsEqual

Factors $n = p \cdot q$ when $p$ and $q$ share both their $s$ most
significant bits and their $r$ least significant bits, with $r + s$ at least
roughly one quarter of the bit length of $n$.

This check combines two observations about $a = (p + q) / 2$:

1.  If the $s$ most significant bits of $p$ and $q$ are equal, then
    approximately $2s$ of the most significant bits of $a$ match
    $\lfloor \sqrt{n} \rfloor$.
2.  If the $r$ least significant bits of $p$ and $q$ are equal, then
    $p \equiv q \pmod{2^r}$, which implies
    $((p - q) / 2)^2 \equiv 0 \pmod{2^{2r - 2}}$ and therefore

$$n \equiv \left(\frac{p + q}{2}\right)^2 \pmod{2^{2r - 2}}.$$

By computing both the integer square root $\lceil \sqrt{n} \rceil$ and the
2-adic modular square roots $\pm n^{1/2} \bmod 2^{k+1}$ (where
$k = \lfloor (\text{bits} + 1) / 2 \rfloor$), the algorithm splices the high
bits from $\lceil \sqrt{n} \rceil$ with the low bits from the modular square
root and searches over the remaining `middle_bits` (default 3 bits) to test
whether $s^2 - n$ is a square. Factored keys are flagged with
`SEVERITY_CRITICAL`.

### CheckOpensslDenylist

Checks whether the RSA modulus appears in the denylist of weak keys produced by
the predictable Debian OpenSSL PRNG vulnerability (CVE-2008-0166), where the
entropy pool was accidentally reduced to the process ID (`getpid()`). Matching
keys are flagged with `SEVERITY_CRITICAL`.

### CheckContinuedFractions

Computes the continued fraction expansion of $n / 2^{\text{bits}}$ (where
$\text{bits} = \text{bit_length}(n)$) to detect RSA keys whose prime factors
contain repetitive bit patterns or are close to rational multiples of a power of
two, as observed in [SmartFacts].

If both $p$ and $q$ are close to small rational fractions times
$2^{\text{bits}/2}$, then $n = p \cdot q$ is close to
$(u / v) \cdot 2^{\text{bits}}$ for a convergent $u / v$ of
$n / 2^{\text{bits}}$, which manifests as an abnormally large partial quotient
in the continued fraction expansion. At each step of the Euclidean algorithm for
$\gcd(n, 2^{\text{bits}})$:

1.  Using the continued fraction identity
    $u_1 \cdot 2^{\text{bits}} - v_1 \cdot n = (-1)^k \cdot \text{rem}$, the
    check forms $n \cdot v_1 = u_1 \cdot 2^{\text{bits}} \pm \text{rem}$ in
    $O(1)$ shifts and additions without tracking $v_1$ explicitly.
2.  It decomposes $n \cdot v_1 = a x^2 + b x + c$ in balanced base
    $x = 2^{\lfloor \text{bits} / 2 \rfloor}$ with $b, c \in [-x/2, x/2)$
    and tests whether the quadratic discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4 a c$ is a
    perfect square, in which case $\gcd(n, 2 a x + b \pm \sqrt{\Delta})$
    splits $n$.
3.  When $n$ has an odd bit length (for example, a 2047-bit modulus produced
    by two 1024-bit primes where the top bit was not forced to `0b11`), the
    prime bit size is $(\text{bits} + 1) / 2$ rather than
    $\lfloor \text{bits} / 2 \rfloor$. In that case, the check also tests the
    quadratic decomposition in base
    $x_{\text{hi}} = 2^{(\text{bits} + 1) / 2}$ for both $n \cdot v_1$ and
    $2 n \cdot v_1$.
4.  Even if the quadratic factorization does not split $n$, the check flags
    the key as weak if any partial quotient in the continued fraction exceeds
    `bound` (default $2^{48}$, chosen via the Gauss-Kuzmin distribution so
    that testing billions of random 2048-bit keys yields a negligible false
    positive probability).

Keys failing this check are flagged with `SEVERITY_CRITICAL`.

### CheckBitPatterns

Attempts to factor $n$ when at least one prime factor $p$ contains a
repeating bit pattern of period $s$, so that

$$p = \frac{a \cdot 2^k + c}{2^s - 1}$$

for small integers $a$ and $c$ (even when some of the most significant and
least significant bits of $p$ deviate from the repeating pattern).

For each candidate pattern period $s$ (covering odd lengths
$1, 3, \dots, 15$, Mersenne-like LFSR periods $31, 63, 127, 255, 511$, and
power-of-two word sizes $8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256$ up to
$\text{bit_length}(n) / 8$), the check invokes `rsa_util.CheckFraction(n, d)`
with $d = 2^s - 1$. `CheckFraction` writes $n = u w + v$ with
$w = 2^{\lfloor \text{bits}/2 \rfloor}$ and reduces the 3-dimensional lattice

$$L = \left[\begin{matrix}
x & 0 & u d \bmod w \\
0 & x & v d \bmod w \\
0 & 0 & w
\end{matrix}\right]$$

using LLL (where $x = 2^{\text{bit_length}(d)}$). Because $d p = a w + c$,
the lattice contains a short vector $(c x, -a x, (c d u - a d v) \bmod w)$,
from which $\gcd(a x w + c x, n) = p$ recovers the prime factor. Factored keys
are flagged with `SEVERITY_CRITICAL`.

### CheckPermutedBitPatterns

Extends `CheckBitPatterns` to prime factors whose repeating bit pattern has had
adjacent words of size $w \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}$ swapped due to endianness or
word-ordering bugs, as observed in several smart-card keys in [SmartFacts].

Swapping $w$-bit words in a repeating pattern of period $s$ increases the
apparent period to $s \cdot 2 w$, which can exceed the $\text{bits} / 8$
lattice bound of `CheckBitPatterns`. However, such primes can still be written
as $p = (a \cdot 2^k + b) / d$ using the smaller denominator

$$d = (2^s - 1) \cdot \frac{2^{s \cdot w} + 1}{2^w + 1}.$$

For each word size $w \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}$ and odd pattern size
$3 \le s < w$ with $\text{bit_length}(d) \le \text{bit_length}(n) / 8$, the
check passes $d$ to `rsa_util.CheckFraction(n, d)` to factor $n$
(`SEVERITY_CRITICAL`).

### CheckPollardpm1

Checks whether $n$ can be factored by Pollard's $p - 1$ algorithm (or
Williams' $p + 1$ algorithm when a smooth factor divides $n - 1$).

A prime $p$ is vulnerable to Pollard's $p - 1$ method if $p - 1$ is
powersmooth, meaning it is a product of small primes and prime powers dividing a
precomputed smooth exponent $m$ (by default constructed from primes up to
$2^{20}$ with the first 150 primes raised to powers up to $2^{64}$).

To run at scale across billions of keys, `rsa_util.Pollardpm1` first applies a
fast filter:

$$\gcd(n - 1, m) \ge 2^{60}.$$

When this filter triggers:

1.  **Pollard $p - 1$ step:** Using base $a = 2^{n - 1} \bmod n$, the check
    computes $g = \gcd(a^m - 1 \bmod n, n)$. If $1 < g < n$, $n$ is
    factored immediately.
2.  **Backtracking when $g = n$:** If both $p - 1$ and $q - 1$ divide
    $m$ (so $a^m \equiv 1 \pmod n$ and $g = n$), the check strips factors
    of 2 from $m = 2^t \cdot m_{\text{odd}}$, computes
    $b = a^{m_{\text{odd}}} \bmod n$, and repeatedly squares $b$ while
    checking $\gcd(b - 1, n)$ to split $p$ and $q$ whenever the 2-adic
    valuations of the orders of $a$ modulo $p$ and $q$ differ.
3.  **Williams $p + 1$ Lucas step when $g = 1$:** Because
    $n - 1 = p q - 1 = (p + 1)(q + 1) - (p + q)$, a large smooth factor in
    $\gcd(n - 1, m)$ can also arise when $p + 1$ and $q + 1$ share smooth
    factors. When $g = 1$, the check evaluates the Lucas sequence
    $V_m(5, 1) \bmod n$ and tests $\gcd(V_m - 2, n)$ to recover factors
    vulnerable to Williams' $p + 1$ method.

Keys failing this check are flagged with `SEVERITY_CRITICAL`.

### CheckLowHammingWeight

Checks whether $n = p \cdot q$ is a product of primes with abnormally low
Hamming weight (sparse binary representations).

The check performs a best-first search from the most significant bits of $p$
and $q$ down to the least significant bits using a bucket priority queue
(Dial's algorithm). Partial guesses $(p_0, q_0)$ with $b$ remaining bits and
combined Hamming weight $\text{hw}$ are prioritized by the heuristic

$$h(p_0, q_0) = \text{bit_length}(n - p_0 q_0 2^{2b}) + 5 \cdot \text{hw}.$$

For random RSA keys, the minimum heuristic value never drops below
$\text{bit_length}(n)$, allowing the search to terminate after `cutoff = 2500`
steps. If a promising branch is found, the search continues up to
`maxsteps = 10^6` steps:

*   If the exact factors $p$ and $q$ are found, the key is flagged with
    `SEVERITY_CRITICAL`.
*   If the search reaches `maxsteps` without factoring $n$ but discovers a
    partial factorization with heuristic value
    $\le \text{bit_length}(n) - 12$, the key is flagged as potentially weak
    with `SEVERITY_UNKNOWN`.

### CheckUnseededRand

Checks whether the RSA modulus was generated from an unseeded or default-seeded
PRNG by testing precomputed outputs $p_0$ of common unseeded generators. For
each candidate $p_0$ of bit length
$k = \lfloor (\text{bits} + 1) / 2 \rfloor$, the check tests three variants to
account for different prime-MSB conventions in crypto libraries:

*   $p_0$ unmodified,
*   $p_0 \mid 2^{k - 1}$ (most significant bit forced to `0b1`), and
*   $p_0 \mid 2^{k - 1} \mid 2^{k - 2}$ (two most significant bits forced to
    `0b11`).

Each candidate guess is passed to
`special_case_factoring.FactorWithGuess(n, p_1)`, which uses Lehman's
continued-fraction method on $p_1 / \lfloor n / p_1 \rfloor$ to factor $n$
whenever $p_1$ agrees with $p$ on at least roughly $2/3$ of its bits
(`SEVERITY_CRITICAL`).

### CheckSmallUpperDifferences

Checks whether the difference $|p - q|$ is close to a power of two:

$$\Delta = 2^{k - c} \quad \text{for } c \in \{2, 3, 100, 128, 160, 256\},$$

where $k = \lfloor \text{bit_length}(n) / 2 \rfloor$.

FIPS 186-4 requires that $|p - q| > 2^{k - 100}$. An implementation that
misinterprets this requirement might set
$q = \text{next_prime}(p + 2^{k - 100})$ (or use a similar fixed offset such
as $2^{k - 128}$, $2^{k - 160}$, or $2^{k - 256}$), or generate $q$ by
flipping only the second or third most significant bit of $p$
($\Delta = 2^{k - 2}$ or $2^{k - 3}$).

For each candidate difference $\Delta$:

1.  The check computes $s_0 = \lfloor \sqrt{n + (\Delta / 2)^2} \rfloor$ and
    directly tests $s \in \{s_0, s_0 + 1, s_0 + 2\}$ to see if $s^2 - n$ is
    a perfect square (which immediately factors $n$ when
    $|p - q| \approx \Delta$ with small prime gaps, including
    $\Delta = 2^{k - 2}$ and $2^{k - 3}$ where $p_0 = s_0 + \Delta / 2$ is
    too far from $p$ for Lehman's $n^{1/3}$ bound).
2.  It then invokes `FactorWithGuess` with guess $s_0 + \Delta / 2$ to cover
    larger deviations around $\Delta$.

Factored keys are flagged with `SEVERITY_CRITICAL`.

### CheckKeypairDenylist

Checks whether the RSA modulus is affected by the Keypair vulnerability
([Keypair], CVE-2021-41117), where a bug in seed initialization left 29 of the
32 seed bytes as zero. Using a precomputed table indexed by the 64 most
significant bits of $n$, the check reconstructs the 32-byte seed, regenerates
$(p, q)$, and verifies whether $p \cdot q = n$ (detecting approximately
90.6% of vulnerable keys with `SEVERITY_CRITICAL`).

## Aggregate Checks

### CheckGCD

Computes the greatest common divisor of each RSA modulus $n_i$ with the
product of all other moduli in the batch:

$$g_i = \gcd\left(n_i, \prod_{j \neq i} n_j\right).$$

Rather than computing $\binom{N}{2}$ pairwise GCDs in $O(N^2)$ time,
`rsa_util.BatchGCD` builds a binary product tree followed by a remainder tree
([MiningPsAndQs], [RonWasWrong]) to compute all $g_i$ in quasi-linear time.
Any key with $g_i > 1$ shares a prime factor with another key in the batch and
is factored and flagged with `SEVERITY_CRITICAL`.

### CheckGCDN1

Applies `BatchGCD` to the shifted values $n_i - 1$ across a batch of keys and
flags any key where

$$\gcd\left(n_i - 1, \prod_{j \neq i} (n_j - 1)\right) \ge 2^{128}.$$

While a large shared factor in $n_i - 1$ does not directly factor $n_i$, the
probability that two independent random integers share a GCD $\ge 2^{128}$ is
negligible. This heuristic check (`SEVERITY_UNKNOWN`) is designed to surface:

*   ROCA-like prime generators where $p - 1$ and $q - 1$ share a large fixed
    divisor,
*   Provable prime generators (e.g., Maurer's algorithm) or strong prime
    generators that reuse intermediate prime factors of $p - 1$ across
    different keys.
